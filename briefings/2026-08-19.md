## Domestic US Security

Federal authorities have issued urgent warnings regarding active cyber threats targeting critical infrastructure. The FBI and CISA specifically highlighted an AI-backed campaign utilizing AI-generated code to compromise critical infrastructure controllers. This campaign is reportedly targeting vulnerable Siemens S7 devices, with officials emphasizing that the risk is no longer theoretical. In a move to curb foreign influence, the Pentagon has threatened to cut federal funding for 30 universities due to their foreign academic partnerships. Simultaneously, Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) has brought voter fraud charges against a Chinese national. However, the DHS is reportedly unable to provide evidence for previous claims it made regarding noncitizen voting. In New York Harbor, a Coast Guard investigation into a boat capsizing that killed a woman and an infant led to the arrest of an illegal alien. Internal friction at the Department of Defense is evident as the longtime publisher of Stars and Stripes announced their departure, citing differences with Pentagon leadership. Legal and administrative issues persist within the DHS, including an ethics complaint against a DHS lawyer for social media posts targeting judges. Additionally, Trump officials have scrapped a previous initiative to recruit Pentagon lawyers to represent migrant children. Finally, reports suggest concerns regarding the DHS's responsiveness to congressional inquiries from associates of Mullin.

## China / Taiwan

Taiwan's Cabinet has approved a record-breaking defense budget proposal for 2027, totaling US$35 billion. This spending represents 3.01% of the island's GDP, a move driven by growing pressure from the People's Liberation Army (PLA). Military preparations are focusing on critical geography, specifically the defense of a key river that could prove decisive in a battle for Taipei. Beyond formal military structures, some residents in a polarized Taiwan are engaging in independent training to defend their homeland. In the United States, defense analysts are calling for a two-year sprint to rebuild the American arsenal to ensure continued deterrence against China. This urgency is coupled with observations that the U.S. is sailing through the Taiwan Strait less frequently, leading to debates over whether this shift impacts regional security. On the diplomatic front, China has issued praise for Malaysian Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim following his supportive comments on Taiwan. These shifts highlight the ongoing geopolitical volatility surrounding the South China Sea and the Taiwan Strait. The combination of record spending and tactical readiness suggests Taiwan is bracing for a more contested environment. The U.S. effort to replenish munitions underscores a perceived gap in readiness for a high-intensity conflict. Meanwhile, China continues to seek diplomatic alignment with Southeast Asian partners like Malaysia. Together, these developments indicate a simultaneous escalation in material preparation and diplomatic maneuvering in the region.

## US / Iran

The security landscape between the United States and Iran remains volatile as the ongoing Iran War continues. President Trump has announced a "crushing economic operation" targeting Tehran while diplomatic talks remain in limbo. This coincides with reports that Iran has lost a key economic lifeline in the Middle East that previous sanctions had failed to reach. While Trump asserts that the Iranian economy is collapsing, a former central bank adviser in Tehran has publicly pushed back against this claim. On the military front, the U.S. is currently conducting a stealth operation to transport oil through the Strait of Hormuz. Finally, new data highlights the escalating financial costs associated with the Iran War.
