# Daily Security Briefing — 2026-08-20

## Domestic US Security

Federal authorities, including CISA and the FBI, have issued urgent warnings regarding an active cyber threat targeting U.S. critical infrastructure. Officials emphasize that the risk is no longer theoretical, as attackers are now utilizing AI-generated code to compromise critical infrastructure controllers. A specific AI-backed campaign is currently targeting vulnerable Siemens S7 devices. These developments highlight a shift toward the integration of artificial intelligence in offensive cyber operations against domestic industrial systems. Simultaneously, the Pentagon has fired the publisher, editor, and a reporter from Stars and Stripes, the U.S. military's newspaper. The Department of Defense cited "insubordination" as the primary reason for the dismissals. These actions followed an interview conducted by the staff with CBS. Reports indicate the firings occurred amid a pressure campaign led by Hegseth. The departing publisher issued a parting shot at the Pentagon upon their exit. In separate domestic security matters, the Department of Homeland Security is reportedly unable to provide evidence for a significant claim it made regarding noncitizen voting. Furthermore, reports suggest that DHS has been notably responsive to inquiries from congressional allies of Mullin.

## China / Taiwan

Taiwan has proposed a record-breaking defense budget for 2027, totaling US$35 billion. The Cabinet has officially approved this proposal, which sets defense spending at 3.01% of the nation's GDP. This significant financial increase is a direct response to growing military pressure from the People's Liberation Army (PLA). Simultaneously, the US Navy conducted aircraft transits through the Taiwan Strait. These maneuvers are taking place ahead of a planned meeting between Chinese President Xi Jinping and US President Trump. This activity underscores continued US commitment to the region amid high-level diplomatic tensions. On the tactical front, reports indicate that Taiwan's forces are intensifying preparations for the defense of Taipei. Specifically, military planning is focusing on the strategic importance of defending a key river to prevent an adversary's advance into the capital. This suggests a focused effort on hardening specific geographic chokepoints to thwart an invasion. The combination of record spending and targeted tactical readiness signals Taiwan's intent to strengthen its deterrence capabilities. The US Navy's presence serves as a visible signal of support during a volatile geopolitical window. Together, these developments highlight a heightened state of security readiness across the Taiwan Strait.

## Russia / Ukraine

Military analysis indicates that Russian ballistic missiles are increasingly successful in penetrating Ukrainian air defenses. In response, reports reveal that Ukraine planned to swarm Moscow airports using AI-guided drones. Ukraine has also reported that North Korea is expanding its military support for Russia to include the deployment of drone operators. Domestically, President Zelenskyy fired a top aide amid a graft probe, while Ukraine's former defense minister is demanding elections. Within Russia, experts are warning of an impending social crisis as the death toll from the conflict continues to rise. Geopolitical risks are mounting as signs of "war fatigue" emerge across Europe. ## US / Iran

Six months into the ongoing war with Iran, the Trump administration has escalated its strategy by announcing a "crushing economic operation" aimed at the Iranian state. This campaign, described as an economic "D-Day," is intended to collapse Iran's economy, though Tehran has already issued a sharp rebuttal to the threat. While President Trump asserts that Iran's economy is currently collapsing, a former central bank adviser in Tehran has publicly pushed back against these claims. Simultaneously, the U.S. is conducting a stealth operation to transport oil through the Strait of Hormuz to bypass Iranian interference. This operation occurs amid shifting dynamics in the Strait, where data indicates varying levels of willingness among shipping vessels to defy either U.S. or Iranian authority. In the broader region, the Houthis are ramping up activity across Yemen and Saudi Arabia, although they have thus far avoided reigniting the Yemeni civil war. Intelligence suggests that Iranian hardliners are intensifying their efforts, with recent warnings signaling that their offensive posture is only increasing. Analysts describe the current state of affairs as "managed instability," noting the potential for prolonged conflicts in both Iran and Ukraine. Despite the economic pressure, reports indicate that Iran has not yet folded under the weight of U.S. sanctions and military action. In a separate development, newly released emails reveal a controversial collaboration between ICE and Iran to deport Iranian nationals from the United States. The financial toll of the conflict continues to mount, with new reports detailing the escalating costs of the war. These developments come as diplomatic talks remain in limbo, leaving the economic campaign as the primary U.S. lever.
