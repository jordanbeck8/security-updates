Together, these developments illustrate a volatile security environment characterized by record naval posturing, strategic economic warfare, and shifting diplomatic alignments.

## US / Iran

The United States has launched a massive financial offensive against Iran, characterized by President Trump as an "economic D-Day." Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent unveiled new sanctions specifically designed to block Iran's revenue streams and isolate the regime. This initiative, referred to as "Operation Economic Outcast," aims to force third-party countries to sever all financial ties with Tehran. The administration describes the move as the "greatest financial offensive" ever deployed against the Islamic Republic. In response, Iran has dismissed the measures as the actions of a "desperate" United States and claimed the sanctions will fail. Tehran has further escalated tensions by threatening the seizure of ships. These threats coincide with an ongoing Hormuz crisis, which analysts warn hides a deeper oil threat that could persist beyond the current conflict. Domestically, the financial cost of the ongoing Iran war has become a point of significant political contention. Democrats are demanding detailed spending reports from President Trump and Secretary Hegseth. Simultaneously, a billboard campaign in military cities is attempting to link the costs of the Iran war to rising consumer prices. The Center for American Progress has also raised questions regarding U.S. spending priorities in relation to the conflict. Overall, the security landscape is currently defined by a shift toward the total economic isolation of Iran coupled with heightened risks of maritime instability.
