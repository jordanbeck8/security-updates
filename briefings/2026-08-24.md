## Domestic US Security

CISA has imposed its most stringent three-day patching deadline to date to address a "perfect-10" Oracle vulnerability. This urgent directive comes amid growing political scrutiny regarding the agency's operational capacity. Lawmakers are calling for an investigation into workforce cuts at CISA, with House Democrats specifically requesting a GAO study to determine the impact of these reductions. Within the Department of Defense, concerns over censorship and press freedom have intensified. A Pentagon spokesperson admitted to the existence of a secret blacklist targeting journalists. This admission coincides with reports that a military newspaper editor was terminated after raising concerns about Pentagon censorship. These developments highlight a period of internal friction within the U.S. security apparatus, spanning from workforce instability at CISA to transparency issues at the Pentagon. The combination of critical software vulnerabilities and reduced agency staffing presents a heightened risk to national cyber resilience. Overall, the day's activities reflect a focus on aggressive immigration enforcement and a contentious relationship between the defense establishment and the press.

## China / Taiwan

This shift in communication is occurring in anticipation of a scheduled summit between President Xi Jinping and Donald Trump. Meanwhile, regional diplomatic efforts are underway as Japanese lawmakers visited Beijing. The visit aims to mend ties and ease tensions following fallout from remarks made by Takaichi regarding Taiwan. These developments reflect a volatile environment characterized by escalating military posturing, critical technology protection, and high-stakes diplomatic maneuvering.

## Russia / Ukraine

During these meetings, President Zelenskyy sought a significant boost in air defense capabilities, although one key backer was notably absent from the anniversary gathering. In a major defense policy development, the European Commission approved €6.1 billion specifically for Ukraine's defense. Zelenskyy also indicated a strategic window for potential U.S. mediation exists until the summer of 2027. On the operational front, Ukraine is intensifying economic pressure by targeting Russian retail giants, with Ozon identified as the next primary target. Simultaneously, reports indicate a shift in Russian military leadership through a series of new promotions. Former Vice President Mike Pence argued that stopping ballistic attacks would effectively remove the last fighting tool from Vladimir Putin’s hands. Additionally, analysis from the Atlantic Council suggests the conflict will persist until Putin accepts Ukrainian independence. These developments underscore a combination of increased financial support and a hardening of Ukraine's political stance against internal calls for elections.

## US / Iran

The United States has significantly escalated its economic campaign against Iran, implementing a strategy termed "Economic D-Day." Scott Bessent announced new sanctions specifically aimed at blocking Iranian revenue streams to exert maximum pressure. This economic offensive is designed to achieve strategic objectives that previous negotiations and airstrikes were unable to secure. In direct response to the widening of these sanctions, Iran has vowed to retaliate. Consequently, the operational focus of the conflict is reportedly shifting toward Iraq. Regarding maritime security, President Trump announced that mines have been fully removed from the Strait of Hormuz. This development follows a critical military preparation in which the Army NETCOM transferred network and C2 services between theaters for the first time prior to the war. The administration continues to tighten economic screws even as some analysts suggest Iran may be numb to financial pain. The shift in the battleground to Iraq indicates a rising geopolitical risk for U.S. assets and allies in the region. Overall, the current security environment is defined by a transition toward total economic warfare combined with the stabilization of key maritime corridors.
