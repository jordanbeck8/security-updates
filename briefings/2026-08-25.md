## China / Taiwan

Taiwan is significantly ramping up its defense capabilities as the president pushes for the passage of a $6.6 billion military drone bill ahead of a crucial vote. This legislative push coincides with military assessments suggesting that China currently lacks the capability to hold a beachhead on the island. On the intelligence and trade security front, Taiwanese authorities have charged nine individuals in connection with the smuggling of high-end AI servers to China. These arrests highlight ongoing vulnerabilities in the protection of critical dual-use technology. Geopolitically, analysts suggest Beijing is pursuing a strategy of "unification without formal unification" to erode Taiwan's autonomy. China is also intensifying diplomatic pressure on Eswatini, Taiwan’s last remaining ally in Africa, by ordering its citizens to evacuate the country. Meanwhile, regional dynamics are shifting as Japan appears to be attempting to rebuild bridges with China following previous disputes over Taiwan. The ripple effects of Taiwan Strait tensions are also being felt further south, with emerging security concerns impacting Thailand. Collectively, these developments indicate a multifaceted Chinese approach combining diplomatic isolation, technological theft, and strategic pressure.

## US / Iran

Six months into the ongoing war with Iran, the United States is pivoting its strategic approach toward intensified economic pressure. Marco Rubio has informed allies that the U.S. is shifting its primary focus from military strikes to an expanded sanctions regime. As part of this "economic war," the U.S. is attempting to revive a Civil War-era court to legally claim Iranian oil as a prize. Simultaneously, U.S. lawmakers are pressuring the Treasury Department to target Chinese banks that facilitate Iranian financial transactions. In the Persian Gulf, the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps (IRGC) announced that Iran and Oman have agreed to share revenue from the Strait of Hormuz. However, Iran has warned that this potential deal could include a ban on military ships entering the strait. Tehran has explicitly accused the United States of obstructing the Hormuz agreement during ongoing talks with Oman. These developments contribute to a conflict that analysts now characterize as "energy trench warfare." Internally, the Iranian government is facing mounting instability, evidenced by widespread labor protests and panic buying. The Institute for the Study of War provided a detailed update on these security dynamics on August 25, 2026. This shift in U.S. policy reflects a strategic gamble that economic strangulation can achieve objectives that neither airstrikes nor negotiations previously could. Ultimately, the U.S. appears to be searching for new options as the conflict reaches a six-month stalemate.
