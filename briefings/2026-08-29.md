## China / Taiwan

Security tensions between China and Taiwan remain elevated as military preparations and geopolitical friction intensify. Reports indicate that troops stationed on islands critical to Taiwan's defense are currently preparing for the possibility of a Chinese first strike. This urgency is underscored by expert warnings that the People's Liberation Army (PLA) is shifting toward "systems warfare," a strategic transition that necessitates a fundamental change in Taiwan’s defensive posture. To bolster these capabilities, a Taiwan-bound Harpoon Coastal Defense System recently scored a direct hit during a US Navy test, signaling continued US military support. These developments follow a period of significant maritime pressure, with Chinese government ships spotted off Taiwan in record numbers during July. On the diplomatic front, the Pacific Summit has opened amid a contentious row over Taiwan, highlighting the issue's central role in regional stability. Simultaneously, Taiwan's Mainland Affairs Council (MAC) has issued a specific advisory urging "high-risk nationals" to exercise caution when traveling to China. Further security context is provided by the Institute for the Study of War in its latest update on the cross-strait situation. The convergence of PLA strategic evolution and increased naval activity suggests a heightened risk of escalation. Consequently, the focus for Taiwan's military remains on hardening defenses on key outlying islands. The integration of advanced US weaponry like the Harpoon system is seen as a critical component of this deterrence strategy. Together, these factors point to a volatile security environment characterized by both tactical preparations and diplomatic instability.
