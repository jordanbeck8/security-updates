## China / Taiwan

**BLUF:** Chinese President Xi Jinping urged U.S. President Donald Trump to take the next step in opposing Taiwan independence during their summit, a request that drew immediate criticism from Taipei officials (South China Morning Post, The Japan Times, Reuters).

**Key developments:**
- Chinese President Xi Jinping pressed U.S. President Donald Trump to take the next step on Taiwan by opposing independence, while Trump hailed China as a World War II ally (South China Post, The Japan Times).
- A Taipei official stated that arming Taiwan remains an important U.S. interest following the Trump-Xi summit (Reuters).
- Taipei officials described Beijing as the “true disruptor” after Xi raised the Taiwan issue with Trump (The Jerusalem Post).
- Taiwan expressed concern over Xi’s pressure on Trump to change the U.S. stance on the island (Reuters).
- Taiwan independence, alongside AI, trade, and Iran, topped the agenda at the Trump-Xi summit (PBS).
- Trump’s warm welcome for Xi was met with caution and uncertainty in Taiwan (South China Morning Post).

**Analyst comment:**
Taiwanese officials likely view Xi’s public appeal to Trump as a coercive diplomatic tactic designed to isolate Taipei and pressure Washington, rather than a genuine shift in Beijing’s strategic posture (moderate confidence). The primary alternative explanation is that Xi is testing the limits of Trump’s willingness to enforce the One-China Policy more strictly against Taipei’s de facto independence, leveraging the summit’s positive atmosphere to extract concessions (low confidence). This judgment depends on the assumption that Taipei interprets Beijing’s public diplomacy as a direct threat to its security architecture, which is supported by the immediate, sharp rebuttals from Taipei officials (high confidence). No link is reported between this diplomatic pressure and specific military movements or economic sanctions in the current reporting.

**Gaps / indicators:**
- Monitor for specific U.S. policy adjustments or legislative changes regarding arms sales to Taiwan in the weeks following the summit, which would confirm whether Xi’s pressure influenced Washington’s operational stance.
- Watch for PLA naval or air activity patterns in the Taiwan Strait during the immediate post-summit period to distinguish between routine training and potential signaling in response to the diplomatic friction.
